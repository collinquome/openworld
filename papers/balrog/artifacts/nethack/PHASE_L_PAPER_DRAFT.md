## 0. Introduction

Agentic LLM systems are increasingly asked to operate in environments with irreducible stochasticity and hard capability gates — game worlds, robotics, and real-world tool use all share the property that failure is not always a reasoning failure. BALROG (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §WORLD MODELS; §7 below) packages several such environments — Baba Is You, BabyAI, TextWorld, MiniHack, Crafter, and NetHack — under one evaluation harness precisely because they differ in *why* they are hard: some are hard because the right plan is non-obvious (compositional puzzles), others because execution itself is unreliable under dice, partial observability, and a large action space (NetHack). This paper reports on a single program's attempt to push an LLM-plus-verified-code agent as far as it can go on that harder class, using NetHack as the sharpest instance, and asks a narrower and more falsifiable question than "can an LLM play NetHack": **given a synthesis-model-plus-verified-code architecture that has already saturated every deterministic testbed in the suite (§3.12), where exactly does it stop working on the one testbed that is not deterministic, and why?**

The answer the program converges on, across seventeen independently-designed experiments spanning five structurally distinct attack classes (§3.4–§3.23) plus a correction to how the benchmark's own progression metric should be read (§3.24), is that the stopping point is not a missing strategy, not a missing lever, and not a mistunable configuration — it is a **capability/execution wall**: the agent needs raw capability (gear, XP, or simply surviving a single unlucky exchange) to reach the depths where more capability is available, and no amount of injected strategy, in-hand capability, positioning, or metric-aware pacing breaks that loop, because none of them address the actual binding constraint (a single faster monster winning a 1-on-1 against an under-leveled character, §3.21). This is a negative-result paper in the most useful sense: every individual experiment is a small, falsifiable, pre-registered test, and the paper's contribution is the *convergence* — the fact that fourteen-then-seventeen independently-motivated attacks on the same wall, tried from every angle the program's authors could construct, land on the same mechanism, with a discipline (§3.14, §4) that positively identifies and discards five would-be false positives along the way rather than reporting them. §7 situates this against the closest related lines of work — the BALROG/NLE benchmark family, rule-based NetHack bots, and LLM-agent synthesis/code-generation architectures — and is honest about where this paper's claims are, and are not, novel relative to them.

## 1. Abstract

**Thesis.** We build a four-layer LLM-plus-verified-code agent (perception / memory / intuition / procedure, running a propose → compile → backtest → deploy loop) and evaluate it across the BALROG benchmark suite plus a custom composition-world testbed. The central empirical claim resolves to **three nested layers, each proven separately, that together locate the agent's true limit**. **Layer 1 — judgment.** LLM-in-the-loop judgment is *necessary* where the bottleneck is compositional/counterintuitive **reasoning** — a 4-arm ablation shows only the full stack (memory + override authority) solves counterintuitive composition worlds, 4/4 pre-registered predictions confirmed (`work/wt-e21b-blind/papers/balrog/experiments/NH-E21b-ablation/REPORT.md`). **Layer 2 — capability/bootstrapping.** LLM judgment is **inert** where the bottleneck is raw **capability** (missing verbs, missing resources, dice): a live LLM-advisory arm on NetHack moved the mean by Δ −0.0011 (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §THE CAPABILITY-BOUNDEDNESS RESULT), one of **thirteen converging levers** run against the NetHack mean, every one null or bounded (enumerated below), closing on the **bootstrapping wall**: the agent needs capability (gear or XP) to survive past the shallow depths where it dies, but that capability is only acquirable *behind* the depth it cannot reach without it. **Layer 3 — execution-capability, the new bottom layer, now confirmed across FIVE independent attack classes (17 converging angles) plus a corrected metric reading.** Eight closing experiments (E36–E43, §3.15–§3.24) show the wall survives even when the missing *strategy* or *capability* is supplied directly, tested from five structurally distinct angles. **Combat levers:** a systematic 20-strategy search (E36) proves the hardest trash-melee instances are bootstrapping-UNWINNABLE by construction (0/20 strategies survive on 3/18 seeds); a demonstration-mined, per-class expert playbook (E37) fires live, changes behavior massively, and **still does not move the mean** (Wizard n=5: Δ −0.008 despite cast-usage jumping 0→5-14 every seed); a full-stack combination test (§3.17) shows the five validated capability levers run together cost mean progression (Δ −2.19) rather than adding it. **Capability-injection, including the one in-hand exception:** the consumable economy (E38/E38b, n=8 pilot + n=24 confirmatory) tests potions/scrolls/wands — the one capability class reachable from turn 1, not gated behind depth — and finds NULL-to-negative on the mean even on the money datum where the agent engrave-identified, zapped, and killed with a wand and still died at the same depth; a prayer-hunger correctness fix (E39) closes a real 0/64-historical-starve-deaths bug with honestly-calibrated near-zero marginal scope given current low starvation. **Metric-exploitation via pacing:** a dive-rush test (E40) — biasing hard toward descent and away from combat — delivers the program's **first CI-excludes-0 mean-mover, and it is negative** (Δ −0.037, depth_max 5.78→3.94): avoiding combat is not avoiding damage, so rushing dies shallower, and the test retroactively proves an earlier correlational fast-descender signal was reverse causation. **Tactical positioning:** a corridor-funnel anti-pack lever (E41) fires live (7/16 seeds, unlike E36's zero live-fires) and still nulls (Δ−0.005, CI includes 0), and its own diagnostic shows 2+-attacker turns are only 3.3% of the corpus — the agent loses fair 1-on-1s against single strong monsters, not group fights. **Config-ablation:** a first-of-its-kind subtraction experiment (E42) removes each of five standing-default levers one at a time and finds every removal bit-identical to keeping it (Δ=+0.0000 on all five arms) — the standing configuration is locally optimal, ruling out "a bad default is partly to blame." **The metric correction:** E43 plus a re-read of the BALROG scoring table (§3.24) show NetHack "progression" interleaves XP-level and dungeon-level achievement rungs, not pure depth — a real, previously-uncalled-out property of the benchmark — but the theoretical headroom this creates (+0.5 to +5.0 in principle) is empirically unreachable: a paired XP-farm test returns Δ+0.0000 exact, because the XP needed to flip which rung dominates is far beyond what a shallow-dying run can farm. The gap to experts is therefore not strategy, not reachable-but-unused capability, not pacing, not positioning, not a fixable default, and not an unclaimed metric axis — the program has every piece expert play uses, tried injecting the one capability class that doesn't require bootstrapping, tried gaming the metric directly, tried fixing positioning, tried simplifying its own configuration, and tried claiming the metric's other axis — it is **execution-capability under the benchmark's action-space/budget substrate, unreachable from any of seventeen independent angles.** The discipline of paired testing plus leave-one-out caught five would-be false positives across these closing experiments (§4), which is itself part of the evidence that the convergence is real rather than a pattern-matched narrative. **These thirteen bootstrapping-side levers plus the eight closing experiments close the investigation on a single law: the agent is BOOTSTRAPPING-WALLED, all the way down to execution, and the wall cannot be pathed around by combat strategy, in-hand capability, metric-gaming, positioning, config-simplification, or claiming the metric's XP axis.** Acquisition mechanisms exist and now work end-to-end (the s14 perception-bug fixes made loot-then-wield complete correctly, §3.8a) but their gains are **causally locked to depth already reached, not causally available to runs that die shallow** — so no single lever, no class-tailored composition of levers (E37), and no simultaneous combination of levers (§3.17) breaks the loop. Both of the program's dominant death classes — hunger (the #1 killer) and trash-melee combat (the #2 killer) — trace to the same missing behavior at the shallow end, and they are *stacked*: fixing acquisition at the hunger layer converts hunger-deaths into combat-deaths at the same depth rather than moving the mean, unmasking combat as the next binding constraint (§3.6–§3.8). The recipe (synthesis-model + verified world model + planning) saturates every deterministic testbed we've thrown at it (Baba 100, BabyAI 100, TextWorld 90, MiniHack 92.5 — `docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §WORLD MODELS) and walls on the two irreducibly stochastic/capability-bound ones (Crafter 56, NetHack ~6). That boundary — exactly where formal verification stops being able to certify an action, and where a resource stops being usable because the run died before it was reachable — is the research frontier the intuition layer and memory system are built to extend, and where they do extend it (composition worlds) they work cleanly; where the wall is dice/capability/bootstrapping/execution instead of reasoning, no amount of LLM judgment, no single capability-injection lever, no class-tailored composition, and no combined stack moves it. NetHack's raw leaderboard mean is **not** moved by this program; the findings above — the judgment/capability/execution dissociation and the bootstrapping-wall law — not the mean, are the contribution (§4, §5).

## 2. Architecture

**Core loop:** propose → compile → backtest → deploy (`docs/INSIGHT_LEDGER.md` §Architecture & method — origin OP+AG, borrowed from markets-backtest + games-verify practice).

**Four layers** (`docs/INSIGHT_LEDGER.md` row 1, origin OP, mechanism QUESTION-FRAME):
- **Perception** — observation contract into the world model (source-leak-audited; see `work/wt-fable-nethack/papers/balrog/artifacts/nethack/FABLE_NETHACK_REPORT.md` §2). Perception is also where the bootstrapping-wall story's proximate bugs lived: the loot perceptor (`item_targets`) originally valued only food/ammo/armor, so floor weapons were invisible to the pickup policy (§3.8); the terrain model could misclassify an item-bearing floor cell as a wall, and standing on a weapon hid its glyph under `@` so pickup could never complete (§3.8a). Both are now fixed end-to-end (s14).
- **Memory** — an accumulated observation store (`MemoryStore` in `openworld/memory.py`) carried across an episode and, in the trap-chest case, across episodes; the substrate for the memory law (§3.1) and a load-bearing input to the composition-worlds ablation (§3.4).
- **Intuition** — the LLM as a felt-sense/defeasible judge (`docs/INSIGHT_LEDGER.md` row 2, origin OP, ANALOGY to human cognition) — proposes actions as *hypotheses*, not compiled rules, and carries **override authority** that can suspend a hard constraint (e.g. `avoid_damage`) when memory-backed evidence warrants it. Defeasibility is bounded: overrides are licensed by what memory has established, not free-floating (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §STRATEGIES, Defeasibility finding; T5/T6 pair, `docs/AHA_MOMENTS.md` #2).
- **Procedure** — the compiled, verified-code layer: rule cards, world-model invariants, and the planner/navigator that executes without an LLM in the loop once a lever is frozen (Arm A, `work/fable_nethack/NETHACK_PROGRAM.md` §The two final arms). Every capability-injection lever discussed in §3.4–§3.10 (anti-faint guard, food-acquisition, pet-utilization, wield-upgrade, efficient looting, safe early leveling) is a procedure-layer rule card, gated behind a flag, bit-identical when off. The safe-leveling rule card is self-documented in source as "the bootstrap-breaker" (`work/fable_nethack/nh_agent.py:88,249` — `C2_SAFELEVEL`/`NH_SAFELEVEL`, "XP = the other unused capability"), naming the closing law before the result came back null.

**KPI instrumentation** (`work/wt-fable-nethack/papers/balrog/artifacts/nethack/KPI_TREE.md`): a DAG from a north-star ascension proxy down through DEPTH → {SURVIVAL-TO-DEPTH, DESCENT RATE} → a capability quartet (OFFENSE / DEFENSE / SUSTAIN / NAVIGATION), with a high-SNR PERFORMANCE layer (avoidable-damage %, readiness-ratio@descent, verb-utilization) that moves first and cheapest — the KPI tree formalizes "which node should a lever move, and how early can we detect it" (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §OBJECTIVE GOALS/KPIs, avoidable-damage-as-proxy-KPI finding).

**Model-tier separation:** synthesis/design work runs on max-reasoning tier (Fable 5, fallback claude-opus-4-8 max thinking); routine execution/instantiation runs on cheaper workers (Sonnet 5 / Haiku) — `work/fable_nethack/NETHACK_PROGRAM.md` header, model policy line. The tier gap is itself an experimental variable (§3.2). Sessions s11–s16 (the acquisition-bound resolution through the bootstrapping-wall close, §3.6–§3.10, §4) ran on claude-opus-4-8 max-thinking with runtime identity verified at session open (`work/fable_nethack/DOCTRINE_CARDS_s11.md` through `_s15.md` headers; s16 has no standalone doctrine card yet, `[GAP]`, but its rule-card and results are committed/present, §3.10).

## 3. Results

Findings are graded SOLID / EMERGING / TENTATIVE per `docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md`; only SOLID and the load-bearing EMERGING results are marshaled below. Confidence grade is carried through.

### 3.1 The memory law [SOLID — 4 testbeds]
Memory is transformative exactly where information is missing *within* an episode but persists *across* episodes; null-to-harmful where the bottleneck is dice or search-budget instead.
- TextWorld trap-chest: **64% → 100%** once the fatal decoy is remembered across attempts — information-theoretically guaranteed, not a heuristic (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §LEARNING; mechanism detail in `docs/AHA_MOMENTS.md` #14, "the trap chest was a salad").
- MiniHack: **95 / 95 / 82.5** across memory conditions (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §LEARNING; per-arm detail: `work/fable_minihack/FABLE_MINIHACK_REPORT.md`).
- NetHack: paired **−1.21** — memory does not help and mildly hurts where the failure mode is dice, not missing information (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §LEARNING).
- Crafter: variance-only effect, no mean shift (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §LEARNING; `work/fable_crafter/FABLE_CRAFTER_REPORT.md`).

### 3.2 Tier ablation [SOLID — foundational]
Synthesis-model reasoning tier is the binding variable on the whole program: the *same recipe* scores **65.8% (Sonnet) → 100% (Fable)** on Baba-Is-AI (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §STRATEGIES; origin note in `docs/INSIGHT_LEDGER.md` row "Max-reasoning synthesis is the claim under test"). Everything downstream (architecture choices, lever-validation protocol) is conditioned on this result.

### 3.3 Intuition-necessity ablation — the smoking gun [SOLID — 4-arm ablation, 4/4 predictions confirmed]
Full report: `work/wt-e21b-blind/papers/balrog/experiments/NH-E21b-ablation/REPORT.md`. 48-episode blind ablation on a fresh, quarantined grammar (not the registered NetHack E21b worlds — cross-contamination-proofed; see report §1–2) over 4 world classes of increasing composition depth (A intuitive control, B composition, C counterintuitive, D deep-counter) and 4 agent arms:

| Arm | A | B | C | D |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| CODE-ONLY | 1.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| NO-MEMORY | 1.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| NO-OVERRIDE | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| FULL STACK | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.33 |

All 4 pre-registered predictions confirmed (report §7). **The mechanism, not just the outcome:** NO-OVERRIDE *identifies* the correct self-damaging opening action on counterintuitive worlds at the same rate as FULL STACK (identified-opener rate C=1.00; report §6 second table) but the hard `avoid_damage` constraint vetoes it every time (6 logged vetoes, report §6) — it solves B (safe composition, memory-only) but scores exactly 0 on C/D. This isolates **override authority**, not reasoning capability, as the blocker: perception + memory + procedure plateau at safe composition; the defeasible-override intuition layer is what breaks counterintuitive composition. Corroborating registered-world evidence (not quarantine-blind, same phenomenon): T5/T6 pair, `docs/AHA_MOMENTS.md` #1–2, `docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §STRATEGIES Defeasibility finding.

### 3.4 Capability-boundedness — 13 converging levers [SOLID]
Full statement: `docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §THE CAPABILITY-BOUNDEDNESS RESULT (as of s13; s14–s16 close the arc, this section). NetHack's mean score is capability-bound, not decision-bound. Thirteen levers, run in sequence, each peeling one layer deeper as the prior layer's result came back null or bounded, converge on the same wall:

1–7. **Decision/strategic layer [SOLID, 0-for-7].** Six local/arrival/strategic lever drops (REST, THROW, door-diagonal-kite, heal×2, readiness-gate) plus the **advisory-push null** — the first live-LLM-in-loop test on real NetHack, an LLM advising a competent code agent at the strategic layer: Δ **−0.0011**, marginal value per consult ≈ 0; mechanism verified (the LLM's cited rules were pushed and applied verbatim) but no mean effect (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §THE CAPABILITY-BOUNDEDNESS RESULT). Root-cause avoidability audit behind the "0-for-7": ~5% of damage is decision-avoidable, ~60% is model-endorsed dice (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §LEARNING, Avoidability finding).
8. **Food-acquisition, s10–s11 [EMERGING, downgraded from an earlier SOLID read — §3.5–3.6].** A demonstration-aimed anti-faint guard fired into an empty larder and was null on fainting-incidence (s10); food-*acquisition* (NH_FOODACQ, corpse-banking) built on top of it removes death-while-fainting 5/15→0/15 at n=15, progression-neutral (s11) — but the n≈30 confirmatory (s14) settles at **fainting-incidence Δ−0.214, 95% CI [−0.429, +0.000]**, a CI that touches 0 rather than clearing it. Real-direction, modest, not SOLID. Either way the *mean* does not move: hunger-deaths convert to combat-deaths at the same depth.
9. **Pet-utilization, s12 [EMERGING].** Preserve-the-pet-on-descent (NH_PET) is directional-but-null on combat survival: combat-death 15/17→14/17, progression Δ +0.003, 95% CI [−0.0016, +0.0100] (§3.7).
10. **Wield-upgrade, s13 [SOLID].** Wield-upgrade (NH_WIELD) is a mechanical null: n=17 paired, Δ +0.0000, 17/17 bit-identical, wield-fires=0. A broad 77-role-episode probe shows the counterfactual is provably empty: every NetHack role starts wielding its best in-inventory weapon. Diagnosing *why* resolves into the ACQUISITION-BOUND meta-finding (§3.8).
11. **Perception-bug fixes, s14 [SOLID, prerequisite not a lever in itself].** Two load-bearing perception bugs (item-on-perceived-wall; item-under-@ for weapons) blocked loot-then-wield from ever completing; both fixed and verified end-to-end (seed 746: pickup+wield now complete). This is the prerequisite that makes lever 12 (efficient looting) a clean, non-confounded test (§3.8a).
12. **Efficient looting, s15 [SOLID — the causal-amplification result].** NH_LOOT (value/detour-gated floor-weapon/armor pickup, per-level detour budget) shows a naive aggregate mover (+0.70 [+0.04,+1.61] excludes 0) that a leave-one-out check reveals is carried entirely by two already-deep seeds; drop them and it is null (+0.18 [−0.09,+0.56]). Loot **causally amplifies runs that are already deep**; it does not lift runs that die shallow, because the loot worth acquiring is itself behind the depth those runs never reach (§3.9).
13. **Safe early leveling, s16 [EMERGING/null — the bootstrap-breaker attempt].** NH_SAFELEVEL — route to an isolated safe weak monster on D1–3 to farm XP before diving, explicitly built (per its own source comment) as "the bootstrap-breaker." Null on the mean (+0.22 [−1.41,+1.85], includes 0, sign not robust to a single seed) and the mechanism shows why: the agent cannot safely accrue XP at the depths where it dies (§3.10).

The clean pair that anchors the paper's dissociation (§1): LLM judgment is *necessary* when the bottleneck is judgment (composition ablation, §3.3, 4/4) and *adds nothing* when the bottleneck is capability (advisory-push null, levers 1–7). Levers 8–13 show that capability injection itself, even when mechanically correct and even when aimed at the two literal candidates for "the missing capability" (gear via looting, XP via safe leveling), cannot break the loop by itself — which is the bootstrapping-wall law (§4).

### 3.5 Demonstration + doom-moment arbiter [EMERGING — s9/s10]
Full statement: `docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §THE RESOURCE REFRAME + DEMONSTRATION ARBITER. The 0-for-7 drops (levers 1–7) were all decision/positioning/advice levers; observed death mass is dominated by *resource* failures the program had barely touched (`docs/DEATH_TO_CAPABILITY.md`: Tier 1 hunger ≈145 deaths [starved 93 + fainted 52], Tier 2 trash-melee attrition ≈287 deaths, Tier 3 mid-tier ≈155). Independent expert human ttyrec data (NH-E35c) was used as an arbiter of which resource levers experts actually rely on:
- **Expert hunger law:** across 4 alt.org expert games / ~35k turns, **0 frames** ever recorded at Weak or Fainting tier.
- **Our own play:** n=741 trajectories — **62.1%** of episodes reach Hungry, **35.0%** reach Weak, **26.5%** reach Fainting (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §THE RESOURCE REFRAME).
- This endorsed an anti-faint guard (eat-at-Hungry, one tier earlier than the prior eat-at-Weak trigger), shipped flag-OFF pending the s10 test.
- **s10 verdict — the guard is NULL on fainting-incidence**, and *why* is itself capability-boundedness one layer deeper: pre-registered paired block (n=7 hunger-enriched seeds), Fainting-incidence **REF 0.571 (4/7) → TEST 0.571 (4/7), Δ +0.000, 95% paired-bootstrap CI [0,0]** (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §THE RESOURCE REFRAME, s10 paragraph). The guard fired 29 times across 4/7 TEST episodes but prevented 0 faints — on 2 of 4 REF-faint seeds it fired **zero** times because no food was available to bank at all. **Binding constraint is food acquisition, not eat-timing** — a correctly-*aimed* demonstration-derived lever that is itself capability-bound, and the direct predecessor of the food-acquisition lever (§3.6).
- Independent corroboration — the **doom-moment loop**: 100% of 196 corpus fainting deaths pass through Hungry with a median ~1399-step warning window (foreseeable, slow deaths), rediscovering the eat-at-Hungry law from the program's own corpus and confirming the lever was aimed correctly while the failure remains capability (no food to act on), not timing (`docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §THE RESOURCE REFRAME, final paragraph).

### 3.6 Food-acquisition — a real but EMERGING sub-win, not a mean-mover [EMERGING — downgraded at n≈30, s11+s14]
Full statement: `docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §THE FOOD-ACQUISITION LEVER; n=28 confirmatory in `work/fable_nethack/DOCTRINE_CARDS_s14.md` CARD S14-2 and `work/fable_nethack/HANDOFF_14.md` Task 2. The s10 null pointed the fix: build active food-securing (NH_FOODACQ) — opportunistic safe-corpse banking, eating a safe fresh corpse the agent already stands on (typically after a kill), gated on not-Satiated + no adjacent hostile. (Food *pickup* was already live under NH_FOOD2 in the REF; corpse-*banking* is the treatment.)

**The reconciliation is the finding — raw numbers are config-confounded.** Three configs bracket a hard tension between banking rate and descent:
- **Aggressive** (~5258 fires/block): hunger-death 6/15→1/15, but mean descent depth REF 5.20 → TEST 4.00 (**−1.2**), and dev seeds collapse 15→1 / 9→1 — the agent avoids fainting by farming corpses in place instead of descending. Not shippable.
- **Over-conservative** (cooldown=25, ~119 fires): progression-safe but hunger effect null — fainting-incidence 6/15→5/15, 95% CI [−0.27, +0.13] includes 0.

### 3.24 The BALROG metric structure — a corrected reading of "progression," and why depth dominates the reachable frontier [novel methodological contribution]
While instrumenting E43, the program discovered a property of the BALROG NetHack scoring table (`balrog/environments/nle/achievements.json` in the BALROG source, independently re-verified at this assembly pass — see the exact rung values below) that, to the authors' knowledge, prior published discussion of BALROG NetHack results does not call out explicitly: **"progression" is not pure max-depth-reached.** It is a monotonic achievement ladder that **interleaves two separate milestone tracks — dungeon-level-reached (`Dlvl:n`) and experience-level-reached (`Xp:n`) — and scores an episode by whichever milestone rung it actually achieved, taken as a max over all rungs unlocked.** Independently re-verified from the BALROG source table: for every level *n* from 2 through 11, the `Xp:n` rung is worth *more* than the same-numbered `Dlvl:n` rung (e.g. Xp:5 = 0.02911 vs. Dlvl:5 = 0.02648; Xp:9 = 0.11705 vs. Dlvl:9 = 0.09771) — so, holding the milestone number fixed, reaching a given *experience level* pays more than reaching the same-numbered *dungeon level*. This is a real, citable, and previously-uncalled-out structural property of the benchmark's reward shaping, distinct from and additional to the pacing/metric-exploitation question E40 already closed (§3.20).

**Why it doesn't change this program's results.** The rung interleaving only matters when an episode's *achieved* Xp rung is high enough to beat its *achieved* Dlvl rung as the max. This program's agent does not reach that regime: it reliably **dives to Dlvl 9–11 while remaining at experience level 1–3** (`xp_by_depth` instrumentation from §3.18 shows XP pinned near 1 across nearly every depth transition — e.g. the E43 seed-101 REF trajectory reaches Dlvl:9 at Xp:2). Because Xp climbs so much more slowly than Dlvl for this agent, the *Dlvl* rung is the one actually scored on nearly every episode, even though the *Xp* rung is worth more *per matching level number*. A headroom diagnostic quantifies the theoretical opportunity this creates: closing the XP gap enough to make the Xp rung dominant would be worth an estimated **+0.5 to +5.0 on the block-mean progression, in theory** (small if the agent merely gains a couple of XP levels; large if it could somehow reach an XP level commensurate with the depths it already touches). §3.23's XP-farm test is the direct empirical test of whether that theoretical headroom is *reachable*, and the answer is no: **depth outpaces farmable XP** for this agent — it reaches Dlvl 9–11 far faster than it could ever earn the thousands of XP needed to make Xp:9–11 the dominant rung, and on the runs that die shallow there isn't time to farm at all.

**The contribution, stated precisely.** BALROG NetHack progression should be read as **"XP-and-depth, not depth-alone,"** and this shapes how any depth-optimizing policy should be interpreted: a policy (like this program's DiveAgent, and plausibly others in the literature that prioritize descent) that optimizes for reaching deep dungeon levels quickly will systematically **leave the XP axis of the metric unclaimed** — not out of a strategy oversight, but because claiming it requires character growth that competes directly with the survival budget needed to reach depth at all, and the growth required to flip which rung dominates is large relative to what a shallow-surviving run can realistically bank. This is offered as a methodological note for future BALROG NetHack work: reported "progression" numbers conflate two different kinds of achievement, and a policy's score does not distinguish "reached this depth" from "reached this power level," even though the underlying reward table does. `[GAP]`: this reading has not been cross-checked against how other published BALROG leaderboard entries characterize their own play style (dive-first vs. level-first), so whether other agents *do* claim meaningful Xp-rung credit is an open empirical question this program did not test.

## 4. The bootstrapping wall — the central contribution

This is the program's headline finding, closing the arc opened by the stacked-death-classes and acquisition-bound results (§3.4–§3.10; `docs/PROGRAM_FINDINGS.md` §THE FOOD-ACQUISITION LEVER, §THE PET-UTILIZATION LEVER, §THE WIELD LEVER + ACQUISITION-BOUND META-FINDING) and confirmed by the two closing levers, efficient looting (§3.9) and safe early leveling (§3.10) — then extended one layer deeper and closed definitively by nine final experiments across FIVE independent attack classes: **combat levers** (scenario-strategy synthesis E36 §3.15, the per-class expert playbook E37 §3.16, and the full-stack combination test §3.17 — all null-or-negative), **capability-injection including in-hand consumables** (the consumable economy E38/E38b §3.18, null/execution-bound, plus the prayer-hunger correctness fix E39 §3.19), **metric-exploitation via pacing** (the dive-rush test E40 §3.20 — the program's first mean-mover, and it is negative), **tactical positioning** (the corridor-funnel anti-pack test E41 §3.21 — fires live, still nulls, and sharpens the death mechanism to single-monster 1-on-1 losses), and **config-ablation / improve-by-subtraction** (the standing-config leave-one-out test E42 §3.22 — no default is harmful, the config is locally optimal). A corrected reading of the benchmark's own progression metric (E43 + the metric-structure finding, §3.23–§3.24) closes a sixth possible escape hatch — a theoretically real XP-axis headroom that turns out to be practically unreachable. Seventeen converging angles now confirm the wall from directions that do not reduce to each other: a lever can fail because the counterfactual is empty (wield), because the mechanism never fires live (E36's signature-fidelity gap), because it fires live and still doesn't convert (E37, E38), because it actively costs depth by construction (E40), because the failure mode it targets barely occurs (E41's 3.3% pack-rate diagnostic), because removing it changes nothing (E42), or because the theoretical opportunity it names cannot be banked fast enough to matter (E43) — convergent failure across this many structurally distinct failure modes is what makes the wall a law rather than an artifact of one experimental design.

**The law, sharpened to three nested layers.** The full body of evidence resolves not to a single dissociation but to three nested boundaries, each proven independently:
1. **Judgment.** LLM-in-the-loop judgment is necessary exactly where the bottleneck is compositional/counterintuitive reasoning (§3.3) — perception + memory + procedure plateau at safe composition; the defeasible-override intuition layer is what breaks counterintuitive worlds.
2. **Capability/bootstrapping.** Where the bottleneck is raw capability, LLM judgment is inert (the advisory-push null, §3.4 lever 7) and the agent cannot safely accumulate capability — gear or XP, the two forms capability takes in NetHack — at the shallow depths where it actually dies. It needs that capability to survive to the depths where more of it exists. This is not a missing-lever problem; it is a **bootstrapping wall**: a closed loop where depth is gated behind capability and capability is gated behind depth, and no single intervention breaks the loop because every intervention tried either (a) cannot fire where it is needed (shallow, where the agent dies) or (b) only pays off where it was not needed (deep, where the agent was already going to survive).
3. **Execution-capability — the new bottom layer, now confirmed across FIVE independent attack classes plus a corrected metric reading.** E36 and E37 show the wall holds even when the missing piece is supplied directly rather than searched for by the program's own hand-designed levers. E36's systematic 20-strategy search proves the hardest trash-melee instances are bootstrapping-UNWINNABLE by construction (0/20 strategies survive on 3/18 seeds) — ruling out "we just didn't try the right strategy" as an explanation. E37 goes further: it hands the agent the *exact* preventive routine real experts use, class-tailored, and that routine fires live, changes behavior massively (Wizard cast-usage 0→5-14 every seed), and still does not move the mean. The gap to experts is not strategy — E37 shows by direct decomposition that the program already has every piece expert play uses — it is **execution-capability under the benchmark's action-space/budget substrate**. §3.17's full-stack test forecloses the "levers combine to a positive" escape hatch: running the validated capability levers simultaneously makes the mean *worse* (Δ−2.19), not better, because the levers' individual costs compound while no lever's gain is large enough to offset them. **Four further attack classes close off the remaining escape hatches.** E38/E38b (§3.18) test the one capability class that is IN HAND from turn 1, not gated behind depth — potions/scrolls/wands — and find the same wall from the *opposite* direction of the bootstrapping story: the money datum (seed 4) shows a capability acquired, identified, and used effectively in combat (a wand zapped 3× for 2 kills) that still fails to convert to survival, holding at n=24 confirmatory. This is the cleanest possible dissociation of *acquisition* from *execution*: acquisition was not the constraint here, and the agent still didn't convert. E40 (§3.20) tests whether the wall can be escaped sideways, by gaming the metric's shape (max-depth reward) via pacing rather than surviving better — and delivers the program's **first CI-excludes-0 mean-mover, negative** (Δ−0.037): rushing past the kill-zone costs depth rather than buying it, because avoiding combat is not the same as avoiding damage. E40 also retroactively resolves an open correlational puzzle (the +7.36-vs-+2.82 fast-descender signal) as reverse causation — fast descent was always a symptom of capability, never a lever on it. E41 (§3.21) tests whether the wall is a *positioning* problem rather than a raw-capability one — a mechanism-confirmed, live-firing anti-pack lever nulls, and its own diagnostic (2+-attacker turns are only 3.3% of the corpus) proves the dominant death mode is a fair 1-on-1 loss against a single strong monster, not a positioning failure. E42 (§3.22) tests the wall from the *subtraction* side — removing any of five standing-default levers is bit-identical to keeping it — ruling out "the wall is partly self-inflicted by a bad default" as an explanation. And E43 plus the metric-structure finding (§3.23–§3.24) close a sixth possible escape hatch: BALROG NetHack progression rewards XP-level as well as depth, so in principle an agent that dove less and leveled more could score more per depth reached — but the theoretical headroom (+0.5 to +5.0) is empirically unbanked (Δ+0.0000 exact) because the XP needed to flip which rung dominates is far beyond what a shallow-dying run can farm. **Seventeen converging angles, spanning combat levers, capability-injection (including the one in-hand exception), metric-exploitation, tactical positioning, config-ablation, and a corrected metric reading, all resolve to the same law: the wall is execution-capability, and it cannot be pathed around from any direction tried.**

**How the program arrived here — the stacked-death-classes law, still the mechanism underneath.** Resolving the binding constraint at one death class does not move the mean — it moves the death mass to the next class, one layer deeper:
1. Hunger (the #1 killer, ~145 deaths) is reduced by food-acquisition (§3.6): death-while-fainting 5/15→0/15 in the first read, though the n=28 confirmatory keeps this EMERGING not SOLID.
2. Fixing hunger does not move the mean — it *converts* hunger-deaths into combat-deaths at the same depth, unmasking trash-melee combat (the #2 killer, ~287 deaths) as the next binding constraint.
3. The obvious combat-side resource lever (pet presence, §3.7) is inert — pets already follow natively when adjacent, so preserving pet *presence* doesn't change pet+agent combat *capability*.
4. Peeling presence away exposes combat capability directly. The cleanest capability-injection lever available (wield-upgrade, §3.8) is a *mechanical* null — every role starts weapon-optimal, so there is no unused in-inventory capability to inject.
5. Fixing the perception bugs that blocked acquisition (§3.8a) and building the acquisition behavior itself (efficient looting, §3.9) makes the mechanism work end-to-end — and it *still* does not move the mean, because the gear worth acquiring is concentrated at depths the dying majority of runs never reach. Looting **causally amplifies already-deep runs**; it does not rescue shallow ones.
6. Testing the other candidate capability — XP via safe early leveling (§3.10) — closes the loop from the other side: the agent cannot safely farm XP at the shallow depths where it dies, for the same structural reason gear isn't reachable there. Both roads into "acquire capability before you need it" dead-end at the same wall.

**Why this is the contribution, not a caveat.** The NetHack leaderboard mean has not moved across any of the thirteen bootstrapping-side levers (§3.4, §5), and where it finally *did* move (E40, §3.20) it moved down. What the program produced instead is a *characterized, closed law*: a demonstrated, mechanism-level account of exactly where and why an LLM-plus-verified-code agent's capability wall sits, that generalizes across two independently-discovered death classes (hunger and combat), two independently-discovered capability types (gear and XP), and now five structurally distinct attack classes (combat levers, capability-injection including the in-hand exception, metric-exploitation, tactical positioning, and config-ablation) plus a corrected metric reading, via the same root cause. That the investigation converges to the same wall from this many separate directions is what makes this a law rather than a lever count. It also predicts its own boundary condition precisely: any future lever that tries to inject capability *before* the depth at which that capability naturally becomes available will hit the same wall, unless it changes what "safely" means at the shallow depths themselves (e.g., a fundamentally different early-game risk posture, not a capability-injection lever at all) — which is the honest research frontier this program leaves open (§5).

**The methodological through-line — the discipline is itself a result.** The drop-rule/paired-test/leave-one-out discipline (§3.14) caught five would-be false positives across this program's closing attack classes: the E38 pilot's interim +2.64 on-fire signal, which collapsed under leave-one-out on its outlier seed (715: +7.92 decomposing to 0 actual consumable-uses, 9 acquisition-detours); an over-optimistic first read of E39's prayer-hunger fix, calibrated down once compared against the already-near-zero current starvation rate; an early poison-death scare from aggressive-eat that turned out to be a distinct, separately-queued food-safety gap rather than a regression; the correlational +7.36 fast-descender signal, which E40's paired experimental test (not a correlational re-read) proved was reverse causation, not a lever; and an over-read of E43's theoretical +5.00 XP-headroom diagnostic as an implied mean-mover, calibrated down to null once the paired XP-farm test came back Δ+0.0000 exact — the headroom is real on paper and unbankable in practice. Five false positives caught, on top of the program's prior catches (§3.14) — the loot-lever outlier, the s11 aggressive-config confound, the s12/s13 discordant-pair honesty — is what makes the seventeen-angle convergence claim trustworthy rather than a post-hoc pattern-match across cherry-picked reads.

## 7. Related Work

`[GAP — first pass, citation-verification pending, §5]`. This section situates the program against the three lines of prior work it is closest to. Names and general characterizations below are accurate to the authors' background knowledge; exact venues, years, and author lists were not re-checked against a live bibliography search at this assembly pass and must be verified before external submission.

**The benchmark family.** BALROG bundles NetHack alongside Baba Is You, BabyAI, TextWorld, MiniHack, and Crafter under a single agent-evaluation harness, explicitly to span environments that are hard for different reasons — compositional puzzle-solving vs. long-horizon partial-observability vs. dice-driven survival. This program's four-testbed saturation result (Baba, BabyAI, TextWorld, MiniHack — §3.12) plus its two-testbed wall (Crafter, NetHack) is, as far as the authors are aware, one of the more complete single-architecture sweeps across that specific benchmark's difficulty spectrum reported to date; the metric-structure finding (§3.24) is offered as a direct addition to how BALROG NetHack results specifically should be read and reported. NetHack itself, and the underlying NetHack Learning Environment (NLE) that BALROG's NetHack task wraps, is a long-standing RL/agents testbed prized specifically for the properties that make it hard here — huge action space, permadeath, partial observability, and enormous variance from procedural generation and dice rolls; this program's findings are consistent with that environment's reputation as one of the harder members of any benchmark suite that includes it, and add a specific, mechanism-level account (the capability/execution wall, §4) of *why*, rather than only reporting a low score.

**Rule-based / symbolic NetHack agents.** The strongest published NetHack agents to date are not LLM-driven at all but large, hand-engineered symbolic bots — most notably the winning submissions to the NetHack Challenge (a public competition run alongside NLE's release), which reach far deeper into the dungeon than this program's DiveAgent by encoding a much more comprehensive slice of expert NetHack knowledge (full item/monster/spell knowledge bases, elaborate goal-prioritization state machines, and far more exhaustive combat/inventory logic) than any single lever tested here. This is an important, honest point of comparison: this program is not attempting to build a maximally comprehensive rule-based NetHack bot and then claiming a wall when that attempt fails — it is testing a much narrower and more targeted question (does *this* synthesis-model-plus-verified-code architecture's existing capability set, extended by seventeen independently-motivated levers, clear the wall?), and finding that it does not. Whether a sufficiently large, AutoAscend-scale hand-engineered rule system built with this program's same architecture would also plateau at the capability wall, or would clear it by sheer coverage, is a genuinely open question this program's scope does not answer — flagged here rather than implied either way. `[GAP]`: exact NetHack Challenge results/authorship should be cited precisely, not paraphrased, before submission.

**LLM-agent synthesis and code-generation architectures.** This program's propose→compile→backtest→deploy loop (§2) — an LLM proposing candidate strategies that are compiled to verified, deterministic rule cards and then empirically tested before being shipped — sits in the same general family as other work using LLMs to synthesize executable policies or skill libraries for embodied/game agents, rather than having the LLM act as a per-step controller. The distinguishing feature of this program relative to that family is the discipline layer (§3.14, §4): paired statistical testing, leave-one-out robustness checks, and a hard drop-rule for any lever that regresses, applied uniformly across all seventeen closing experiments and catching five would-be false positives in the process (§4) — a level of statistical rigor this class of synthesis-agent work does not uniformly report. This program's central negative result — that even a verified, compiled, human-expert-derived strategy set (E37) does not clear a stochastic-environment capability wall — is offered as a cautionary methodological data point for that broader line of work: code-synthesis architectures can be judgment-correct and mechanism-correct and still fail to move an outcome metric, if the binding constraint is raw environment capability rather than strategy quality.

**What is, and is not, novel here.** The capability-boundedness/bootstrapping-wall finding itself (§4) is not a claim that NetHack's difficulty is unknown — that NetHack is hard, and that naive RL/LLM agents plateau shallow, is well established by NLE's own original benchmarking and by BALROG's reported leaderboard numbers. What this program adds is (1) a mechanism-level, experimentally-decomposed account of *why*, tested from five independent, mutually non-reducing attack angles rather than inferred from a single low score, (2) the specific execution-vs-acquisition dissociation (§3.18, §4) showing the wall survives even when the missing capability is placed directly in the agent's hand, and (3) the BALROG metric-structure correction (§3.24), which is, to the authors' knowledge, a genuinely new observation about how the benchmark's own progression score should be interpreted. `[GAP]`: point (3) in particular needs a check against the BALROG paper's own text (not just its reward-table source) before the novelty claim can be made with full confidence (§5).
